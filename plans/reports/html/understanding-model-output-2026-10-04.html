Understanding model output

A model's answer only helps when the reader can take it in. The output format decides how much work that takes.

tl;dr

  1. Chat text costs the reader the most effort.
  2. Diagrams beat paragraphs for shape and flow.
  3. One HTML file can hold prose, diagrams, and controls.
  4. A video wins when the idea moves over time.
  5. Build the strongest format the question justifies, then throw it away.

Why chat replies fail

A chat reply is linear. The reader scrolls, and the diagram scrolls away.

Facts and asides share one weight. The reader cannot tell what matters.

Nothing moves. The reader cannot open a detail or change a number.

The reader holds the structure in their head, because the page will not hold it for them.

The four rungs

1. Prose

Fast to write. The reader must rebuild the structure from word order.

Cost: minutes. Reader effort: highest.

2. Diagram

Shows shape, flow, and nesting at a glance. Silent on detail.

Cost: minutes to an hour. Reader effort: lower.

3. HTML page

Holds prose, diagrams, tables, and controls in one file. Opens offline.

Cost: an afternoon. Reader effort: lowest for a static artifact.

4. Explainer video

Shows state over time: cause and effect, before and after.

Cost: a day or more. Reader effort: low, but the reader cannot skim.

Write in controlled language

ASD-STE100 is a controlled-language spec. Aerospace teams wrote it for maintenance manuals, where a wrong reading costs a plane.

It forces short sentences, active voice, and one term per concept. You do not need the full certification. Ask for "80 percent of the way to ASD-STE100" and hold the model to a short list:

  • One idea per sentence. Twelve words is normal, twenty is the ceiling.
  • Active voice, present tense.
  • One term per concept, used every time.
  • Fact first, then reason, then exception.
  • Numbers over adjectives.

Ask for a diagram

The ladder above is the pattern. Follow it:

  • One idea per diagram. Split the idea instead of adding to the drawing.
  • Label every part with the name the code or domain uses. The diagram becomes a map.
  • One caption that states the idea.
  • No stock art and no shadows. The drawing carries meaning or it goes.

Ask for an HTML page

This page is the pattern, and a skill in this repo runs it end to end: skills/html-report/SKILL.md.

  • One file with inline CSS and JS. No CDN and no network request. It opens from file://.
  • Light and dark themes, print styles, and a readable measure.
  • Interactive where it teaches. Here the four needs above highlight the matching rung when you pick one.
  • Without JavaScript the content still reads. Controls are a bonus, never the content.
When the idea moves over time, ask for a video

Ask for a narrated explainer, for example: "Create a 3b1b style video explainer on X." The recipe:

  • Script 5 to 12 beats. One idea per beat, and a visual change per beat.
  • Narrate with ElevenLabs when an API key is in the environment. Otherwise check for local options: piper, coqui-tts, edge-tts, or espeak-ng.
  • Render with Manim for math, Remotion for React scenes, or plain HTML and ffmpeg for diagrams.
  • Set each scene's duration from its audio, and keep one build.sh that renders from scratch.

Full recipe: skills/html-report/reference/explainer-video.md.

The point

As models do more of the work, more of our work becomes oversight and understanding.

Custom, discardable artifacts help there. A page or a video that would never have paid for itself before now costs an afternoon.

Ask for the format that makes understanding cheapest. Then let the artifact go.
